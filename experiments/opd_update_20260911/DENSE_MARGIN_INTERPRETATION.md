# Dense margin contraction: working interpretation, not a guarantee

The September 13 beta=.01 dense direct experiment scores 52/400 (13%), versus 210/400 clean OPD and 175/400 SFT. Beta=.01 sparse scores 201/400. Beta=.25 dense scores 179/400. These are exploratory, fixed-seed external student experiments; density and contraction interact strongly. They do not establish a general threshold, a deployable defense, or preserved sampling performance.

Let z be the original teacher logits, m the largest eligible logit, and C the selected K strictly secondary candidates. The transformation leaves the eligible maximizers untouched and sets z'_i=m+beta*(z_pi(i)-m) for i in C, where pi reverses candidate order. Protected special and padding logits are unchanged. The normalized modified distribution is q=softmax(z').

For two transformed candidates i,j, q_i/q_j=exp(beta*(z_pi(i)-z_pi(j))). Consequently beta→0 removes relative preferences within C; their probability ratios approach one. The unchanged eligible maximum also approaches the same probability as each transformed candidate. If these K+1 items dominate the partition, the resulting distribution approaches uniform on them, with entropy approximately log(K+1). That dominance is an assumption: outside candidates and protected tokens remain in the partition.

In ideal real arithmetic with beta>0, the original maxima remain maxima. This is compatible with losing most useful probability ranking information: argmax is only one feature of a distribution. Casting logits to FP16 can create ties; an argmax check performed before the cast does not prove the actual cast preserves every decision. Fitting a finite-capacity teacher can also change its argmax. Neither the transformation nor teacher correctness RL proves benchmark non-degradation.

In the simplified fixed-context distillation objective KL(p_student || q), the unconstrained optimum is q. Thus preserving q's argmax alone places almost no bound on the student's sampled or greedy sequence quality after finite training across contexts. Product probabilities compound across a long sequence; even small per-position preference changes can accumulate. Actual on-policy MiniLLM also changes the context distribution, uses a policy-gradient term and a conditional regularizer, and has a finite budget, so this argument explains a possible mechanism rather than predicting the measured 13%.

Sparse intervention can leave many informative process positions untouched. Dense intervention changes both the action-score signal and conditional regularizer throughout eligible process positions. The beta=.01 sparse/dense comparison motivates separating coverage from amplitude, not treating a larger loss coefficient as equivalent to greater coverage. Both arms use known student trajectories and retrospective process boundaries, so these are strength probes without a deployment claim.

For teacher internalization, sampling 32 process positions uniformly and averaging their losses gives an unbiased estimate of that context's dense mean target loss (conditional on the context and frozen target). Selecting only the highest-divergence eight positions estimates a different, selected-position objective. Uniform position sampling still does not fix the mismatch between offline contexts and future students. Only external held-out student tests can assess that.

Next tests: fit the beta=.01 dense target with a low initial interference weight and teacher-preservation objectives; compare against the ongoing beta=.25 dense fits. Keep original teacher distributions, fixed offline negative text and teacher-own correctness as the only teacher training signals. Test student OPD only after freezing the teacher. New cross-student baseline length experiments also help distinguish genuine suppression from artifacts of truncated training rollouts.
